Figure 2 - Pairwise relationships and Spearman rank correlations (corner plot)
File: fig2_corner.png

LAYOUT
A 7 x 7 grid. Rows and columns are the same seven variables, in the same order:
Cement, Slag, Fly ash, Water, Superplasticizer, Age, Strength.
The cell in row i, column j shows the relationship between the row variable and the
column variable. Coarse and fine aggregate are left out to keep the grid readable.

DIAGONAL - histograms
- Each diagonal cell is the distribution of one variable.
- The tall bar at 0 for slag, fly ash and superplasticizer means many mixtures contain
  none of that material (45%, 55% and 37% of specimens respectively).
- Age is on a log axis; the spike is the 28-day group.

LOWER TRIANGLE - scatter plots
- One dot per specimen (1,030 in total). x is the column variable, y is the row
  variable. Age uses a log axis.
- Dot colour = compressive strength (yellow = high, purple = low; right-hand colour bar).
- The bottom row (Strength vs each variable) is the most direct view of how each input
  relates to strength.
- Dots lining up in straight lines are not an error: the data comes from designed
  mixture series (for example, replacing cement with slag at fixed proportions), not
  from random sampling.

UPPER TRIANGLE - Spearman rank correlation
- The number is Spearman's rho for that pair of variables (row x column).
- Colour: red = positive, blue = negative; darker = stronger. Bold numbers have
  |rho| >= 0.3.
- Stars give significance: * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001.
- Strongest correlations with strength: Age (0.60) and Cement (0.48).
- Strongest correlation between inputs: Water and Superplasticizer (-0.69).

WHAT SPEARMAN RHO MEANS
- Each variable is replaced by its rank (smallest = 1, next = 2, ...), and the ordinary
  Pearson correlation of the ranks is computed.
- It measures a monotonic relationship (does Y tend to rise when X rises?), not a
  straight-line one. +1 = perfectly increasing, 0 = no monotonic trend,
  -1 = perfectly decreasing.
- It was used here because age is strongly skewed and several variables have many
  zeros. Ranks are not affected by extreme values or by transforms such as log(age).
- Example: Age vs Strength has Pearson r = 0.33 but Spearman rho = 0.60. Strength rises
  steadily with age but along a curve, so a straight-line measure understates the
  relationship.
