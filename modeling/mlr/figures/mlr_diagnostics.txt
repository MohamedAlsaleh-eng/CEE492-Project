Figure - Test-set diagnostics of the baseline and selected multiple linear regression models
File: mlr_diagnostics.png (vector version mlr_diagnostics.pdf is produced locally; *.pdf is git-ignored)
Code: modeling/mlr/mlr.py, function fig_diagnostics

All three panels use the 201 held-out test specimens (87 mixtures never seen in
training). Open grey circles = baseline model (8 source predictors). Filled blue dots =
selected model (ln(age) + w/b). Residual = observed - predicted, so a NEGATIVE residual
means the model OVERPREDICTED the strength.

PANEL (a) - predicted vs observed
- x-axis: measured strength; y-axis: predicted strength. A perfect model puts every
  point on the diagonal line. Grey band: +/-10 MPa around the diagonal.
- The legend gives each model's test RMSE in MPa (10.4 vs 6.8).
- What to look for: the blue points sit closer to the line. Above about 60 MPa the
  points fall below the line, i.e. strong concretes are underpredicted.

PANEL (b) - residual vs specimen age
- x-axis: age on a log scale. Baseline points are drawn slightly to the left and
  selected points slightly to the right of each test age so they do not overlap.
- Lines with squares: median residual in each age group (3, 7, 14, 28, 56, 90-100,
  180 and 270-365 days; groups with at least 5 specimens), plotted at the group's
  median age.
- What to look for: the grey baseline line is strongly curved - early ages are
  overpredicted and 56-100 days are underpredicted - because a straight line in age
  cannot follow strength gain that slows down. The blue line is nearly flat up to about
  100 days. At 180-365 days both models overpredict, because ln(age) keeps increasing
  while real strength levels off.

PANEL (c) - residual vs predicted strength (selected model only)
- Black curve: LOWESS smoothed trend of the residuals.
- Dashed lines: +/-1.96 SD of all residuals pooled together. If errors were normal with
  constant spread, about 95% of points would lie inside.
- What to look for: the spread of residuals grows with predicted strength
  (heteroscedasticity; Breusch-Pagan p < 0.001), and the trend dips below zero at high
  predictions.
